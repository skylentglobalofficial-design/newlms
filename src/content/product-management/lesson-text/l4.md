# Talking to users without leading them

**Objective:** Turn a leading question into a past-behaviour question and extract a usable quote from the Harbor Desk notes.

**Why this matters:** People agree to products that do not exist. They are more honest about last Saturday.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 3.

**Estimated time:** 45 minutes.

**Case:** [harbor-desk-case.md](/content/product-management/harbor-desk-case.md)

## Explain

A **leading question** smuggles the solution: “Would you use a shared inbox?” Most people say yes to be helpful.

A **past-behaviour question** asks what they already did: “Last time a delivery failed, what did you do in the next ten minutes?”

In this course you do not run live interviews. You work the notes you have. That is a limitation — say it in your research note. You *can* still practise:

- Labelling a quote (who said it)
- Not promoting Arun’s “Gmail” sentence to user evidence
- Not interviewing people who are not in the file

**Who not to pretend you interviewed:** customers at large, a CEO, “the market,” Northwind staff.

Meena has never been asked what “done” looks like for a failed delivery. That absence is evidence of a gap, not proof that Harbor Desk is loved.

## Worked example

Leading: “Would a shared inbox help at open?”

Past behaviour: “Walk me through 06:30 last Saturday — who did you message when the milk was late?”

Priya already answered a version of this: she uses three WhatsApp groups and mutes the weekend one. Usable quote:

> “If the milk is late I need someone at HQ who is actually looking before 7am. The group is full of memes by then.” — Priya, T. Nagar

That quote supports a *job* (late inbound owned before 07:00). It does **not** support “Priya wants Harbor Desk.” She also said she turned notifications off on the last all-hands app.

Karthik is not a store user. His evidence is about **constraint**: a status list is in scope; Gmail is not. Keep him in a separate bucket.

## Practice

1. Rewrite these into past-behaviour questions:
   - “Do you want Harbor Desk?”
   - “Would @mentions reduce chaos?”
2. Copy one Priya quote and one Meena quote. Under each, write: *this is evidence of ____, not of ____.*
3. Write one question you wish someone had asked Meena. Label it **unasked**.

## Expected result

Your rewrites mention a real last time (weekend, truck, delivery), not a hypothetical product. Your “not of” lines should block “therefore build Gmail.”

## Common mistakes

- Treating “I would use it” as if it appeared in the notes (it did not).
- Combining four people into “users say they want an inbox.”
- Asking leading questions in the assignment and calling it research.

## Knowledge check

**Q.** Arun said “like Gmail but for stores.” Is that a user quote about the job?

**A.** It is a stakeholder solution. File it under requested UI. Pair it with Priya and Meena’s past behaviour before you keep it.
