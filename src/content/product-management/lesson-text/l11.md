# Choosing one bet

**Objective:** Score three Harbor Desk options against reach, evidence, and effort, then pick one and say what you are not doing.

**Why this matters:** Two engineers and six weeks cannot build an ERP. A PM who will not cut is not deciding.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 10.

**Estimated time:** 45 minutes.

**Case:** [harbor-desk-case.md](/content/product-management/harbor-desk-case.md)

## Explain

You do not need a proprietary scoring app. Three columns are enough:

- **Reach** — whose weekend is affected (cashiers vs all HQ mail vs every store message)
- **Evidence** — how well the case supports it (quotes + log IDs vs Arun’s SKU belief)
- **Effort** — Karthik’s words, not your optimism

**Kill criteria:** what you would measure in four weeks to stop or continue. If you cannot name it, you are asking for faith.

Scoring is not a math proof. It is a way to make the cut discussable. High/Med/Low is allowed. Fake decimals are not.

If two options tie, the constraint breaks the tie. Gmail loses on effort even if Arun prefers it.

## Worked example

| Option | Reach | Evidence | Effort | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Weekend exception queue | Store exceptions; not all chat | Strong on HD-03/06/09 and Priya/Meena | Med — list + status | Karthik’s “simple list” |
| B Full shared inbox | Every message | Weak — 2025 chat was refused; Priya mutes | High — “Gmail badly” | Stakeholder favourite |
| C Rota + Monday checklist | Same jobs, no new UI | Process might still fail if nobody looks | Low | Honest if software is not worth it |

A reasonable bet: **A**, non-goals include B’s mentions/upload and warehouse. Kill if four weekends later, delivery failures still have no owner.

You could argue **C** if you do not believe software will be watched. Then say what you are *not* starting.

## Practice

Copy the table. Fill High/Med/Low yourself — your ratings may differ from the example. Then write:

1. The bet in one sentence.
2. Three non-goals.
3. Kill criteria in one sentence with a count.

## Expected result

The bet names what ships and what does not. Kill criteria mention weekend exceptions or Monday 10:00, not “happiness.”

## Common mistakes

- Scoring B as Low effort because “inbox templates exist.”
- Picking A+B+C as phases so you never say no.
- Adding ML triage as option D.

## Knowledge check

**Q.** Arun hates your cut. What do you owe him?

**A.** The table, the constraint, and the quotes — not a quieter “yes.” You do not owe a secret second roadmap.
