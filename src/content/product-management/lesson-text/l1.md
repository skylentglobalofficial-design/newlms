# What product management is for

**Objective:** Separate product work from delivery theatre — name the decision this course trains you to make.

**Why this matters:** Teams can be busy for months and still not have decided *whose problem is worth the next engineering bet*.

**Prerequisite:** None. A text editor is enough.

**Estimated time:** 40 minutes.

**Case:** [harbor-desk-case.md](/content/product-management/harbor-desk-case.md) — fictional **Harbor Retail**, 12 stores. Not Northwind. Not a sales file.

## Explain

Product management, in this course, is the craft of **choosing a problem** and **choosing one constrained bet**, then writing it down so someone else can build or reject it.

It is **not**:

- Project management (dates, stand-ups, ticket hygiene)
- Visual design
- Writing SQL or training a model
- Promising someone a PM job
- Collecting a certificate

Those are other crafts. Harbor Desk will tempt you toward all of them. HQ already asked for “Gmail for stores.” That sentence is a *solution*. Your job starts one step earlier: **who is failing at what, in what situation, and what would “better” look like without naming the UI.**

Three levels of claim, using Harbor Desk:

| Level | Allowed in this course? | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | Yes | “HD-03 lived on walkie and was forgotten.” |
| Job / outcome | Yes, with care | “Meena needs a place to put a failed delivery so the customer is not her personal WhatsApp.” |
| Unfounded promise | No | “Harbor Desk will raise revenue 12% and get you hired.” |

If a stakeholder wants a revenue forecast, you may say this case has no sales file. Do not borrow Northwind numbers. That is a different course.

**Output** is what the team shipped. **Outcome** is the change in the user’s world. Shipping Harbor Desk v1 is output. “Weekend failures have a written owner before Monday 10:00” is an outcome.

## Worked example

Arun (HQ ops) sent engineering: “requirements: like Gmail but for stores.”

A delivery-minded rewrite: “Build Gmail. Mentions, labels, attachments. Six weeks.”

A product rewrite:

- **User:** Priya at T. Nagar, 06:30, inbound trucks.
- **Job:** Make a late milk truck *someone’s job* before the fridge is empty.
- **Constraint:** Two engineers, six weeks, no warehouse system.
- **Not decided yet:** Whether the object is an inbox, a queue, or a Monday checklist.

Notice the product rewrite does not mention Gmail. You can still pick an inbox later. You have not pretended the solution *is* the problem.

## Practice

Write four sentences in your notes:

1. The decision this course trains (choose a problem + one bet under a constraint).
2. One Harbor Desk observation from the case (include an ID or a name).
3. One outcome you might aim for that does not name “inbox.”
4. One request you would refuse as out of scope for this course (SQL, ML, certificate, job).

## Expected result

Your four sentences should be specific to Harbor Retail. If you could paste them into a Data Analytics lesson unchanged, they are too generic.

## Common mistakes

- Calling “we shipped” the same as “stores are unblocked.”
- Treating Arun’s seniority as evidence quality.
- Importing Northwind, SQL, or Power BI because those exist elsewhere on Skylent.

## Knowledge check

**Q.** A director asks: “Are we being good at product?” Why is that a weak question here?

**A.** “Good” is not a user, a job, or a constraint. Rewrite: whose weekend exception still has no owner, and what would we count in four weeks?
