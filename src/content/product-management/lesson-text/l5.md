# Evidence, not opinions

**Objective:** Sort Harbor Desk claims into observation, inference, and opinion, and name a gap.

**Why this matters:** A backlog of opinions is not discovery. Engineers will ask “how do you know?”

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 4.

**Estimated time:** 45 minutes.

**Case:** [harbor-desk-case.md](/content/product-management/harbor-desk-case.md)

## Explain

Three buckets:

| Bucket | Meaning | Harbor Desk example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | What was seen or said, with a source | HD-09 had no channel; customer WhatsApped the cashier |
| Inference | A reasonable reading you still own | Weekend on-call WhatsApp is a bad home for exceptions because 43 unread buried HD-06 |
| Opinion | Preference or slogan without a handle | “Stores just need to be more disciplined” |

**Frequency vs intensity:** Priya’s milk-truck pain is intense at 06:30. Arun *mentions* missing SKUs but has **no count**. Do not treat intensity and frequency as the same. If you put missing SKUs in the headline problem, you are guessing.

Gaps you must keep visible:

- No prototype test
- No revenue
- No SKU frequency
- Interviews are condensed notes, not recordings

You may still recommend later. You may not launder a gap into a statistic.

## Worked example

Claim: “WhatsApp does not work for Harbor Retail.”

- Observation: Priya mutes the weekend group; HD-06 sat in a 43-unread thread; HD-05 had no channel.
- Inference: Chat-shaped tools failed for *exceptions that need an owner*, which matches the 2025 all-hands refusal.
- Opinion (reject): “Nobody likes WhatsApp.” Meena still uses it with customers because her number is on the bill.

Claim: “Missing SKUs are the core problem.”

- Observation: Arun believes that; HD-02 and HD-07 are missing-SKU rows; he has no count versus delivery failures.
- Honest gap: you cannot rank SKUs vs delivery failures from this file.
- Do not “fix” the gap with a fake pie chart.

## Practice

Sort these five claims. Then add one gap of your own.

1. HD-03 was forgotten after walkie.
2. Harbor Desk will be loved.
3. Arun reconstructs weekends from screenshots on Monday.
4. AI should triage exceptions.
5. Meena forgets customer follow-ups when it is busy.

## Expected result

(1) observation. (2) opinion / unfounded. (3) observation. (4) opinion, out of course scope. (5) observation (her words). Your gap should mention SKU frequency or lack of a prototype.

## Common mistakes

- Averaging four interviews into “80% of users.”
- Using Northwind valid-row counts as if they were Harbor exceptions.
- Hiding gaps in an appendix nobody will read — put them in the synthesis.

## Knowledge check

**Q.** Can you say there were nine weekend exceptions?

**A.** Yes — that is the log in the case. You cannot say that nine is typical, or that it cost ₹X, unless you invent numbers. Do not invent numbers.
