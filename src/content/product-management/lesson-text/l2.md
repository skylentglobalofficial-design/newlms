# Problems, users, and outcomes

**Objective:** Rewrite a vague request into user, job, outcome, and constraint using Harbor Desk.

**Why this matters:** Feature arguments start when the team skipped the user.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 1.

**Estimated time:** 40 minutes.

**Case:** [harbor-desk-case.md](/content/product-management/harbor-desk-case.md)

## Explain

A usable product brief, in this course, has four parts:

1. **User** — a real role in the case (Priya, Meena, Arun), not “the business.”
2. **Job** — what they are trying to get done in a situation (late truck before open; failed delivery while the till is busy).
3. **Outcome** — how you would know the job got easier, in language you could count later.
4. **Constraint** — what you are not allowed to spend (here: 2 engineers, 6 weeks, no ERP).

If any part is missing, people fill it with a UI. “Shared inbox” is what fills a missing job.

**Harbor Retail is not Northwind.** Northwind is a synthetic sales extract used in Data Analytics. Harbor Desk is an operations communication problem. Do not compute category revenue here. There is no CSV that answers “should we build Gmail?”

Refuse a request when it is only a solution, or when the case cannot support it (revenue, hiring, prototype love).

## Worked example

Vague ask: “Stores need better communication.”

| Part | Weak | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User | Stores | Meena, cashier, Anna Nagar |
| Job | Communicate | Put a failed delivery somewhere that is not her personal WhatsApp |
| Outcome | Better comms | Failed deliveries have a written owner the same day |
| Constraint | — | Two engineers, six weeks |

Another vague ask: “Add AI to triage exceptions.”

**Refuse.** This course does not teach models. The case has nine handwritten rows, not a labelled training set.

A third: “Show me which category makes the most money so we know the inbox ROI.”

**Refuse.** Wrong subject. Point them at Data Analytics if they actually have a sales extract. Harbor Desk notes have no net revenue.

## Practice

Rewrite these four asks. For each: user, job, outcome, constraint — or **Refuse** with one reason.

1. “Build Gmail for stores.”
2. “Make weekends less chaotic.”
3. “We should be data-driven about Harbor Desk.”
4. “Guarantee a product job after the capstone.”

## Expected result

- (1) Solution-shaped → rewrite from Priya or Meena’s job, keep the constraint.
- (2) Too vague → pick a weekend exception type from the log (late truck vs delivery failure). You may need two users.
- (3) Slogan → either name a count you could take from the log, or refuse because “data-driven” is not a brief.
- (4) **Refuse.** This product does not place people.

## Common mistakes

- Using “user = Arun” for every rewrite because he wrote the requirements.
- Writing outcomes that are feature lists (“mentions and labels”).
- Inventing a 12th store or a CEO interview.

## Knowledge check

**Q.** Priya says she does not want another chat. Does that kill Harbor Desk?

**A.** It kills “another chat” as the unexamined solution. It does not kill the job “late truck is owned before 07:00.” You still have to choose a bet — which might be a queue, or even a rota, not a chat.
