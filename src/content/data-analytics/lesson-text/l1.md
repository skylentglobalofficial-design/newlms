# What is Data Analytics?

**Objective:** Distinguish descriptive, diagnostic, and speculative work — then compute a defensible net-revenue figure from a dirty sales extract.

**Why this matters:** Stakeholders ask “how are we doing?” Analysts who cannot say *what was measured* produce theatre, not evidence.

**Prerequisite:** None. Spreadsheet or a text editor is enough.

**Estimated time:** 40 minutes.

**Dataset:** [northwind_sales.csv](/content/data-analytics/northwind_sales.csv) — 180 order lines for fictional **Northwind Retail**, January–June 2026.

## Explain

Data analytics is the craft of turning a table into an answer a decision-maker can use.

It is **not** storing files, encrypting disks, training neural networks, or promising someone a job. Those are other crafts. This course trains four practical moves:

1. **Ask** a question the columns can actually answer.
2. **Clean** enough that totals are defensible.
3. **Summarise** with a formula you can write down.
4. **Interpret** in business language, with the limitation stated.

Three levels of claim, using Northwind:

| Level | Allowed in this course? | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Descriptive | Yes | “Valid net revenue in this extract is about ₹8.12 lakh.” |
| Diagnostic | Yes, with care | “April is the weakest month; returns and invalid rows are not the whole story.” |
| Predictive / ML | No | “Next quarter we will do ₹X.” — you do not have a model, and we do not teach one. |

If a stakeholder wants a forecast, you may show the history and say a forecast needs a method this course does not teach.

## Worked example — one order line

Open the CSV. First data row:

`NW-10001, 2026-01-05, South, Chennai, Electronics, Laptop Stand, 1, 2199, 0, 1100, no, store`

Read the fields:

- **units** = 1
- **unit_price** = 2199 (INR list price)
- **discount_pct** = 0
- **returned** = no
- **cost_per_unit** = 1100 (used later for margin, not for headline revenue)

**Net revenue** for a valid sales row in this course:

`net_revenue = units × unit_price × (1 − discount_pct / 100)`

For NW-10001: `1 × 2199 × 1.00 = 2199`.

Now a discounted row, NW-10013 in January (Grocery, Filter Coffee):

`units 7, unit_price 449, discount_pct 10` → `7 × 449 × 0.90 = 2828.7`

**Gross margin** (optional, when the brief asks):

`margin = net_revenue − units × cost_per_unit`

Do not call `unit_price` “revenue”. Price is not a total.

## Step-by-step procedure

1. Download the CSV. Keep a **raw** copy. Never overwrite it.
2. Count rows. You should see **180** data rows plus a header.
3. Scan `returned`, `units`, `unit_price`, `category`, `region`. You are looking for values that would poison a total.
4. Write a **valid-row rule** before you sum anything. The course default:

   - `units > 0`
   - `unit_price > 0`
   - `returned = no`

5. Apply the net-revenue formula only to valid rows.
6. Write one descriptive sentence that includes the metric, the period, and the rule.

Known issues you will hit (do not “fix” the file until a later lesson asks you to):

- Blank `region`: **NW-10024**, **NW-10089**
- Category aliases: **Elec.** (NW-10018), **electronics** (NW-10042 — and the product is Filter Coffee, so the alias is not the only problem)
- Negative units: **NW-10055** (`-2`)
- Zero price: **NW-10122**
- Duplicate `order_id`: **NW-10013** appears in January and March

Using the default rule you should find **166 valid rows**, **14 excluded rows** (12 returns + 1 negative + 1 zero price), and **net revenue ≈ ₹812,020**.

## Practice

Without building a pivot yet:

1. Compute net revenue for **NW-10018** (Desk Lamp, Elec., 3 × 1299, 0% discount, not returned).
2. Decide: does NW-10018 belong in an Electronics total? Write the mapping rule in one line.
3. Decide: does NW-10055 belong in revenue? Why or why not?
4. Write one sentence a commercial lead can use: period, metric, valid-row rule, approximate total.

## Expected result

1. `3 × 1299 × 1 = 3897`.
2. Treat `Elec.` as Electronics **and log the mapping**. Do not leave three spellings in a board pack.
3. Exclude NW-10055 from revenue: `units = -2` fails `units > 0`. Investigate it as a quality issue, not as negative sales, unless operations confirms a reversal process.
4. Example sentence: “For Jan–Jun 2026, Northwind’s valid net revenue is about ₹8.12 lakh, counting only rows with positive units, positive price, and returned = no.”

## Common mistakes

- Summing `unit_price` down the column (that ignores units and discount).
- Keeping `returned = yes` in the headline number.
- Treating `Elec.`, `electronics`, and `Electronics` as three businesses.
- Dropping blank-region rows so the file total and the “clean” total silently diverge.
- Calling this a machine-learning or job-guarantee course.

## Knowledge check

A finance partner says: “Just add all the `unit_price` values — that is revenue.”

What do you do?

**Answer:** Refuse that shortcut. Revenue here is `units × unit_price × (1 − discount_pct/100)` on valid rows. Adding list prices double-counts nothing useful and ignores volume and discount.

Mark this reading complete when you can state the formula, the valid-row rule, and one quality issue from this file.
