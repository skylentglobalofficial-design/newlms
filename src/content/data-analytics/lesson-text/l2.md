# Asking a question the data can answer

**Objective:** Rewrite a vague stakeholder request into a question with a metric, a time window, a segment, and a limitation.

**Why this matters:** Bad questions produce beautiful, useless dashboards. Good questions fit the columns you actually have.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 1 (net revenue formula and valid-row rule).

**Estimated time:** 35 minutes.

**Datasets:** [northwind_sales.csv](/content/data-analytics/northwind_sales.csv), [northwind_hr.csv](/content/data-analytics/northwind_hr.csv)

## Explain

A question the data can answer has four parts:

1. **Metric** — a number with a formula (net revenue, units, headcount, attrition count).
2. **Window** — dates you will include (here: 2026-01-01 to 2026-06-30 for sales).
3. **Segment** — the cut (category, region, channel, department).
4. **Limitation** — what the file cannot tell you.

Template:

> For [window], what is [metric] by [segment], using [rule], and what can we **not** conclude?

Northwind sales **can** answer:

- Net revenue by category / region / month / channel after a stated cleaning rule.
- Whether April is weaker than March in this extract.
- How many rows fail the valid-row rule.

Northwind sales **cannot** answer:

- Customer lifetime value (no customer id).
- “Are we doing well?” (no target, no prior year).
- Next month’s revenue (no forecast method in this course).
- Why a customer returned an item (no return reason).

Northwind HR **can** answer:

- Headcount by department, including a blank department.
- Count of `attrition = yes` (6 people in this file).

Northwind HR **cannot** answer:

- A survival / attrition model (no termination dates, tiny sample).
- Whether attrition “caused” the April sales dip (two files, no join key that proves causation).

## Worked example

**Stakeholder:** “Are Electronics doing well?”

**Rewrite:**

> For January–June 2026, what is valid net revenue for category Electronics (mapping `Elec.` and `electronics` to Electronics), compared with other categories, and how much of Electronics is store vs online?

That question uses columns you have: `order_date`, `category`, `units`, `unit_price`, `discount_pct`, `returned`, `channel`.

**Still illegal:** “Will Electronics beat Apparel next quarter?”

## Step-by-step procedure

When a request arrives:

1. Circle the **verb** (“doing well”, “fix”, “forecast”). Vague verbs need a metric.
2. List **columns** in the CSV. If the metric is not a function of those columns, say so.
3. Fill the template (metric, window, segment, rule, limitation).
4. Propose **one** chart that would answer it — not six.
5. Write the limitation in the same paragraph as the answer so it cannot be edited out.

## Practice

Rewrite each request. If it cannot be answered, say which column is missing.

1. “Show me everything about sales.”
2. “Which region should we close?”
3. “Is remote work killing performance?” (HR file)
4. “What were valid net revenue and units by month?”

## Expected result

1. Too wide. Offer: valid net revenue by category and by month, Jan–Jun 2026, default valid-row rule.
2. Closing a region is a decision, not a finding. You can rank regions by valid net revenue (Unknown is blank region, not a real region to “close”). You cannot recommend closure without cost, lease, and strategy data you do not have.
3. HR has `work_mode` and `performance_rating`, but one rating is `N/A` and this is not a controlled study. You may tabulate average rating by work mode **after** coercing types and dropping/flagging `N/A` — you may not claim remote work “kills” performance.
4. Answerable. Metric: valid net revenue and units. Window: month of `order_date`. Segment: none required. Rule: default valid-row rule.

Reference (rounded): January ≈ ₹1.18 lakh … March peak ≈ ₹1.89 lakh … **April dip ≈ ₹0.92 lakh** … June ≈ ₹1.47 lakh. Use your own sheet; ±1% rounding is fine.

## Common mistakes

- Accepting “dashboard of everything” as a question.
- Answering a decision (“close the North”) with a ranking and no caveat.
- Joining sales to HR on `region` and calling it proof.
- Hiding the limitation in a footnote nobody reads.

## Knowledge check

Which question is ready for this sales file?

A. How do we become a data-driven company?  
B. For Jan–Jun 2026, which category has the highest valid net revenue after mapping aliases?  
C. What will July revenue be?  
D. Which customers are about to churn?

**Answer:** B. A is slogans. C is a forecast. D needs customer-level history.
