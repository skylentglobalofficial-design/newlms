# Pivots, charts, and month-over-month

**Objective:** Build category and month summaries, compute month-over-month change, and draw one chart that matches a written question.

**Why this matters:** Executives remember one comparison. A pivot is how you get that comparison without hand-tallying 180 rows.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 4 working tab with `valid_flag`, `category_std`, `net_revenue`, and dates typed as dates.

**Estimated time:** 50 minutes.

**Dataset:** your working copy of [northwind_sales.csv](/content/data-analytics/northwind_sales.csv)

## Explain

A **pivot table** answers: for each value of a dimension, what is the aggregate of a measure?

- **Dimension** (rows/columns): `category_std`, `region`, month of `order_date`, `channel`
- **Measure** (values): SUM of `net_revenue`, SUM of `units`, COUNT of orders
- **Filter:** `valid_flag` = TRUE, unless you are studying dirty rows on purpose

**Month-over-month (MoM)** for month t:

`MoM% = (value_t − value_t−1) / value_t−1`

If January is the first month, January MoM is **undefined** — not zero. Write “n/a”.

**Chart rule:** one question → one comparison. A bar chart of category net revenue answers “who is biggest?”. A line of monthly net revenue answers “what is the shape of the half-year?”. Do not paste both into a slide titled “Insights”.

## Worked example — category pivot

Question: *Which mapped category holds the largest share of valid net revenue?*

1. Insert a pivot on `work` (valid rows only, or put `valid_flag` in Filters = TRUE).
2. Rows: `category_std`. Values: SUM of `net_revenue`, SUM of `units`.
3. Sort by net revenue descending.

Reference (rounded rupees):

| category_std | valid net revenue |
| --- | ---: |
| Electronics | 347,072 |
| Apparel | 179,739 |
| Home | 155,457 |
| Grocery | 129,752 |

Electronics is first. If your Electronics is much smaller, you probably left `Elec.` and `electronics` unmapped.

**Month pivot**

Add a helper `order_month`:

Sheets: `=TEXT(B2,"YYYY-MM")`  
Excel: `=TEXT(B2,"yyyy-mm")`

Pivot: rows = `order_month`, values = SUM `net_revenue`.

Reference (rounded):

| month | net revenue | MoM |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 2026-01 | 117,967 | n/a |
| 2026-02 | 162,726 | +38% |
| 2026-03 | 188,525 | +16% |
| 2026-04 | 91,729 | **−51%** |
| 2026-05 | 103,899 | +13% |
| 2026-06 | 147,173 | +42% |

The finding is not “sales are fine”. The finding is **April collapsed versus March in this extract**. Diagnostic next steps (not proof): mix shift, fewer valid rows, bigger discount, more returns that month. You will check returns separately because returns are already excluded from this headline.

## Step-by-step procedure

1. Confirm SUM of valid `net_revenue` still ≈ 812,020.
2. Category pivot + sort.
3. Month helper + month pivot + MoM column beside it.
4. Region pivot: blank region → **Unknown** (helper `=IF(C2="","Unknown",C2)`).
5. Chart: clustered bar of category net revenue **or** line of monthly net revenue. Title the chart with the question, not “Chart 1”.
6. Write three bullets: what happened, what you checked, what you still do not know.

## Practice

1. Build the region pivot including Unknown. Which named region is largest? What is Unknown in rupees?
2. Compute April MoM from your month pivot. Does it match the “about −50%” story?
3. Optional: split Electronics monthly. Did Electronics alone cause April?

## Expected result

1. **South** is the largest named region (≈ ₹2.39 lakh). Unknown ≈ **₹5,988** (the two blank-region orders). Dropping blanks would hide that.
2. April MoM is about **−51%** vs March. If you get −5% or +20%, your dates are text or invalid rows leaked in.
3. You will not get a full causal story from one split. Report the Electronics April number and stop at “needs a mix check”, not “the warehouse failed”.

## Common mistakes

- Pivoting `unit_price` instead of `net_revenue`.
- MoM as `value_t / value_t−1` without subtracting 1.
- Putting January MoM = 0.
- Chart with no title question.
- Treating Unknown as South because you filled blanks with the mode.

## Knowledge check

March valid net revenue is 188,525 and April is 91,729. MoM is:

A. 91,729  
B. (91729 − 188525) / 188525 ≈ −51%  
C. 188525 / 91729  
D. 0, because April is “bad”

**Answer:** B.

Mark complete when you have two pivots (category and month), a MoM column with January = n/a, and a chart titled as a question.
